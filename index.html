What happens to your file

The same path the original Python tool takes, rebuilt for the browser.

  1. 01

    Fingerprint

    SHA-256 of your file is written into the header, so the decoder can prove nothing changed.

  2. 02

    Protect

    Reed–Solomon adds 32 parity bytes per 255-byte codeword, then interleaves them so a damaged patch of video doesn't kill one codeword.

  3. 03

    Paint

    Each bit is a cell: white for 1, black for 0. 129,600 cells per frame, 30 frames a second, upscaled to 4K.

  4. 04

    Prove

    On the way back, parity repairs bit errors, the hash is recomputed, and you get a verdict: byte-identical, or not.

Measured, not promised

From the project's own tests, on simulated YouTube compression. 4×4 cells at 4K survived with zero lost bytes — that's the default here.

1 GB works out to roughly 42 minutes of video. It is a real cost, not a footnote.

Before you trust it with anything

There is no promise. A re-encode, a region change, a takedown, and the file is gone. This is a demonstration of error-correcting codes, not a backup.

It is slow and heavy. A gigabyte is ~42 minutes of 4K video. Uploading and downloading takes longer than that.

It may break the rules. YouTube's terms don't invite data storage. Use unlisted videos on a test channel, and never as the only copy.

A drive is cheaper. 15 GB on Google Drive, free, no 4K encode, no waiting for 2160p to appear.
